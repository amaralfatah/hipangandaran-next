The pitch for Batukaras as a digital nomad destination sounds almost too good: surf in the morning,
work in the afternoon, eat cheaply, live slowly. And the pitch is mostly true — with some important
caveats about internet reliability, working hours, and what you're actually giving up by being here
instead of somewhere with a coworking space.

I've been doing this for over a year. Here's what a genuinely productive week looks like, and what
it takes to make it work.

## Before you arrive: what you need sorted

**Internet backup plan.** Don't assume accommodation WiFi will handle your calls. Get a Telkomsel
SIM at the airport or in Pangandaran town. A 30GB package costs Rp 50,000–80,000 and will save you
at least once.

**Accommodation with table space.** "Working from bed" is a sustainable lie for about four days.
Look for homestays or guesthouses that have a desk, a veranda table, or access to a common area
with seating. Ask specifically — many places just have a bed and a fan.

**Work schedule aligned to mornings.** If you need to be available for synchronous calls, try to
stack them in the afternoon (Batukaras time). Morning light is for surfing and the water is calmest
before 10 AM. If your team is in European timezones, afternoon Batukaras = morning Europe — this
actually works beautifully.

## Day 1 — Arrive, settle, don't try to be productive

You've been on a bus or in a car for at least four hours. Give yourself the afternoon.

- **Afternoon:** Check in. Walk the beach. Get your bearings. The bay curves in a way that takes
  a few minutes to understand — the main surf break is to the right (east) looking out from shore.
- **Evening:** Eat at a warung. There's a good one behind the main break that does grilled fish
  and tempe with rice for Rp 30,000. Don't remember the name — never does, but the blue plastic
  chairs are unmistakable. Ask your accommodation where they eat — not where they send tourists.
- **Night:** Test your accommodation WiFi. Run a speed test. If it's under 5 Mbps, decide now
  whether to buy SIM card data for calls tomorrow.

## Day 2 — First surf, first work session

Start the rhythm you'll keep all week.

- **6:00 AM:** Wake up. The light is flat and beautiful.
- **6:30–8:30 AM:** Surf. Conditions are usually best early — offshore winds, cleaner faces. If
  you need a board, most homestays either rent them or know who does (Rp 75,000–100,000 half day).
- **9:00 AM:** Back at accommodation. Shower, breakfast (the standard homestay breakfast — toast,
  eggs, tea — is fine).
- **10:00 AM–1:00 PM:** First proper work block. This is your most focused time. The village is
  quiet; surf was satisfying, not exhausting. Get your most important work done here.
- **1:00–2:00 PM:** Lunch. Warungs are busy now but fast. Nasi campur or mie goreng, Rp 20,000–30,000.
- **2:00–5:00 PM:** Second work block or longer if you need it. Internet at cafes like [Cafe at the
  beach strip] is often better than homestay WiFi — worth relocating for video call-heavy afternoons.
- **5:00 PM:** Sunset. Don't skip this. The bay faces roughly southwest and the light is orange
  through the coconut palms. You didn't come to Batukaras to watch the sunset through your window.

<InfoBox type="tip">
  The local café owners are used to nomads camping for hours. Buy something every 90 minutes or so
  and you'll be left alone indefinitely. The watermelon juice is excellent and costs Rp 20,000.
</InfoBox>

## Day 3 — Same rhythm, different locations

Repeat the morning surf + work pattern but move your afternoon work spot.

- Try working from the common area or veranda of a different café.
- **Evening:** Explore the back roads by scooter. The road behind Batukaras toward Legok Jawa
  passes through rice paddies and is genuinely beautiful at golden hour.

## Day 4 — Green Canyon day (plan around your meetings)

Block a day mid-week for Green Canyon body rafting. The trip takes about 4–5 hours including
transport from Batukaras (~30 minutes each way).

- **Schedule your calls for morning or late afternoon** and leave 9 AM–2 PM free for the trip.
- Green Canyon costs Rp 250,000 per person including guide, life jacket, and helmet. Book through
  your accommodation or directly with operators in Cijulang.
- The body rafting through the limestone gorge is one of those experiences that makes Batukaras
  worth staying near. Do it.
- **Afternoon:** back in Batukaras by 2–3 PM. Light work session. The afternoon after Green Canyon
  is not a writing-intensive afternoon.

See [the full Green Canyon guide](/guides/green-canyon-body-rafting-complete-guide) for details on
what to bring and what the experience is actually like.

## Day 5 — Full work day + Citumang afternoon

- **Morning:** Skip the surf today if you need a proper half-work-day. Or surf briefly and work.
- **Work:** Use your most concentrated block (10 AM–1 PM) for anything that requires deep focus.
- **Afternoon:** Citumang river (about 30 minutes from Batukaras). Different from Green Canyon —
  shallower, calmer, more swimming than rafting. Good for an afternoon off. Rp 150,000/person.
- Citumang has a lovely waterfall section and clear water. It's done in 2–3 hours, leaving you
  back in Batukaras by late afternoon.

## Day 6 — Rest day or explore

By day six you'll know whether you want a rest day or whether you're feeling restless.

**Rest option:** morning surf, slow work afternoon, early dinner, bed at 9 PM.

**Explore option:**

- Scooter day to Pangandaran town (45 minutes east). Walk West Beach — it's longer and sandier
  than it sounds. Visit the Cagar Alam Pananjung edge. Stock up on any supplies.
- Or head to Karapyak Beach (30 minutes west of Batukaras). Quiet, no tourists, good for a swim.
  Not a surf beach but excellent for clearing your head.

## Day 7 — Wind down or extend

If you're leaving, keep it simple — work morning, pack, head out. The bus from Pangandaran town
back toward Jakarta/Bandung runs multiple times daily.

If you're extending — and most people do extend — pick up another week at your accommodation (now
you know enough to negotiate properly) and repeat the rhythm.

## Where to work in Batukaras

| Spot                   | WiFi                  | Noise    | Food/Drink     | Best for          |
| ---------------------- | --------------------- | -------- | -------------- | ----------------- |
| Your accommodation     | Variable              | Quiet    | Limited        | Deep work         |
| Beach strip cafés      | 10–25 Mbps typical    | Moderate | Good           | Calls, browsing   |
| Pangandaran town cafés | 15–40 Mbps            | Busy     | Better variety | Heavy upload days |
| Your phone hotspot     | 5–20 Mbps (Telkomsel) | Anywhere | N/A            | Backup            |

For anything mission-critical — big uploads, client presentation video calls — I go to Pangandaran
town where café WiFi is more consistent. For normal work days, Batukaras is fine.

## The honest tradeoffs

**What Batukaras is great for:**

- Asynchronous-heavy work (writing, design, coding, analysis)
- Roles where you control your hours
- Anyone who values surf and ocean access over urban convenience

**What Batukaras is hard for:**

- Daily video calls requiring stable high-speed internet (doable but stressful)
- Roles requiring last-minute overnight deadlines (power cuts happen, not often but they happen)
- Anything requiring same-day courier service or physical office access

I've found a rhythm here that works. The key is treating the surf window as seriously as you treat
your work window — not as something you do "if time allows," but as the anchor the day is built
around. Once that's set, the rest falls into place.
