I've been in Batukaras for 14 months. In that time I've watched the cost of living creep up slightly
(post-pandemic tourist recovery will do that), but it's still one of the cheapest places in Indonesia
to base yourself if you actually want to surf regularly and not go insane from urban noise.

This isn't a curated "luxury digital nomad" budget. It's a real one, with real numbers I can verify,
and the categories most monthly-budget articles quietly omit.

Prices verified May 2026.

## The one-line summary

A solo digital nomad living comfortably in Batukaras — surfing a few times a week, eating a mix of
warungs and tourist restaurants, renting a scooter — spends **Rp 5–8 million per month**
(roughly **$310–$500 USD**). Budget backpackers can do Rp 3–4 million. Anyone insisting they need
more than Rp 10 million is either paying for a villa they don't need or has a coffee habit.

## Accommodation

This is where Batukaras splits from other surf destinations. There's no Airbnb premium here yet.

| Style                | What you get                         | Monthly cost |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ------------ |
| Budget room          | Fan, shared mandi, basic breakfast   | Rp 1.2–1.8M  |
| Mid homestay         | Private bathroom, AC at night, wifi  | Rp 2.5–4M    |
| Surf camp with board | Room + gear included, community vibe | Rp 3.5–5M    |
| Private bungalow     | Your own space, garden, sea breeze   | Rp 4–7M      |

The real move: negotiate monthly rates. Any accommodation that's even vaguely long-stay-friendly
will drop 25–35% from their daily rate if you commit to a month. I pay Rp 2.8 million/month for a
room with private bathroom, AC (which I use maybe 3 hours a night — breeze handles the rest), and
breakfast. Walking distance to the beach is worth paying a little extra; the rooms furthest inland
get significantly hotter.

<InfoBox type="tip">
  Don't book accommodation online for long stays — walk in person, explain you're staying a month,
  and negotiate face-to-face. Booking.com rates are for people staying three nights. Monthly
  regulars are treated completely differently.
</InfoBox>

## Food

Batukaras has two food universes running in parallel.

**Warung universe (local):** Nasi goreng, mie goreng, gado-gado, grilled fish fresh from the morning
catch. Rp 15,000–35,000 per meal. This is where I eat most lunches. The warung behind the main surf
beach does the best rendang in town; I've had it probably 60 times at this point.

**Tourist restaurant universe:** Smoothie bowls, pasta, "Western breakfast." Rp 50,000–120,000 per
meal. Good occasionally, ruinous as a daily habit.

Realistic monthly food budget for someone who eats local most days but treats themselves twice a week:
**Rp 1.2–2M/month** (Rp 40,000–65,000/day). Add Rp 300–500k if you have a serious coffee habit —
the cafes with good espresso charge Rp 35,000–55,000 per cup.

Groceries exist but are limited. Indomaret in Pangandaran town (25km away) is the nearest
supermarket; I go once a week or once every two weeks for anything I can't get from the market.
The small local warung near the main road stocks basics — eggs, instant noodles, bottled water,
snacks — at honest prices.

## Transport

**Scooter rental:** Rp 75,000–100,000/day, negotiable to Rp 1.8–2.5M/month. You need one.
Batukaras isn't walkable beyond the beach strip, and Pangandaran town, Cijulang, and Green Canyon
all require wheels.

**Fuel:** Rp 10,000–15,000 per tank on a standard 110cc scooter. I fill up twice a week; monthly
fuel cost is around Rp 80,000–120,000 depending on how much I explore.

**Ojek (scooter taxi):** Rp 5,000–20,000 for short local trips. Useful when you don't want to park
or when you've had one too many Bintangs.

**Getting to Bandung / Jakarta:** Rp 100,000–250,000 by bus + minivan depending on the route and
how much you value sleep. Build this into your monthly budget as Rp 200,000–500,000 (one round trip
per month to civilization).

## Surfing costs

This is what most cost-of-living articles skip because most digital nomads writing them don't surf.

| Item                             | Cost                       |
| -------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Board rental (half day)          | Rp 75,000–100,000          |
| Board rental (full day)          | Rp 100,000–150,000         |
| Surf lesson (2 hours)            | Rp 200,000–350,000         |
| Board repair (minor ding)        | Rp 50,000–150,000          |
| Rash guard / wetsuit (rare need) | Rp 150,000–250,000 to rent |

If you're surfing 3x/week on rented boards, add Rp 1.2–1.8M/month to your budget. Buying a
second-hand board (Rp 1.5–3.5M) pays off in about 6 weeks if you surf regularly. I bought a beat-up
7'2" funboard for Rp 2.2M in month two and it's still holding together.

The bay at Batukaras is forgiving — mellow left-hander, sand bottom, suitable from beginner to
intermediate. You're not coming here for serious barrels; you're coming for consistent, uncrowded
waves in a village with a functioning warung 30 meters from the break.

## Internet and SIM cards

Let's be honest about this: Batukaras internet is functional, not impressive.

**Best approach:** Telkomsel SIM, Rp 50,000–100,000 for a 30-day data package (30–50GB). Coverage
at the beach is spotty but workable. Head inland 200 meters and signal improves.

**Cafe WiFi:** The cafes that market to digital nomads typically have 10–25 Mbps. Enough for video
calls if you're not in peak hours (11am–2pm tends to slow down). I've had calls drop maybe once every
two weeks — not bad. Starlink has recently appeared at one accommodation (as of May 2026); I've seen
speeds of 80+ Mbps there, though it's not consistent.

**Monthly internet budget:** Rp 100,000–200,000 for mobile data, zero extra if you work from
accommodations or cafes with included WiFi.

<InfoBox type="warning">
  Don't assume your accommodation's WiFi can carry video calls reliably. Always test on arrival. The
  places that advertise "high-speed WiFi" often mean "WiFi exists." Keep your mobile data loaded as
  backup.
</InfoBox>

## Healthcare and emergencies

Nearest decent clinic: Pangandaran town (~25km). Nearest hospital with any meaningful capacity:
Tasikmalaya (2+ hours). For minor things — cuts from reef, bali belly, sunburn — local pharmacies
(apotek) in Pangandaran stock what you need.

Budget Rp 200,000–500,000/month as a healthcare contingency. Buy travel insurance before you arrive;
it costs less than a single ER visit.

## What's not in this budget

- **Visa extensions:** Indonesia tourist visa is 30 days, extendable once for 30 more days at Rp
  500,000. After 60 days you need to do a visa run (Rp 1.5–2.5M for a Singapore or KL day trip,
  or a B211A visa on arrival from certain countries at ~$45).
- **Big activities:** Green Canyon (Rp 250,000), Citumang (Rp 150,000), boat trips. Budget an extra
  Rp 400,000–800,000/month if you're exploring.
- **Drinking:** Bintang at a tourist bar runs Rp 35,000–55,000. Local arak is cheaper and
  significantly less predictable.

## Is it worth it?

Batukaras makes sense if you surf, or you want to live slowly and cheaply near the ocean, or both.
It doesn't make sense if you need reliable 50+ Mbps internet, regular flights, or a city within
reach without a three-hour bus.

For what it costs — under $500/month for a comfortable, surf-adjacent life by the sea — there's not
much competition in Southeast Asia.
