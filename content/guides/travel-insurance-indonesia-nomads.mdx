I didn't take travel insurance seriously until a friend broke her wrist surfing at Batukaras. The
ER in Pangandaran town (45 minutes away by scooter) didn't have what she needed. She went to
Tasikmalaya. The hospital there was fine but the bill — about Rp 8 million ($500) — came out of
pocket because her insurance policy excluded "adventure activities" including surfing.

She was annoyed. I started reading policies more carefully.

## What actually goes wrong in Indonesia

Before choosing coverage, it helps to know what the real risks are:

**Most common:** motorbike accidents. Indonesia's roads require full attention and most tourists
haven't ridden a scooter in traffic before. Minor accidents — cuts, road rash, fractures — are
routine. The hospital bill for a broken collarbone can run Rp 10–25 million.

**Surf-specific:** reef cuts, shoulder injuries, and the occasional broken wrist. Not exotic; just
common enough to budget for. The reef at Batu Karas is relatively forgiving but still reef.

**GI issues:** bali belly exists in Pangandaran too. Usually self-limiting but dehydration can
escalate. Good insurance means you don't delay seeing a doctor because of cost.

**Dengue fever:** not common in Batukaras specifically but present in the region. Symptoms arrive
fast; hospital observation is sometimes required.

**The rare but real:** appendicitis, motorbike accident requiring surgery, a coral laceration that
gets infected. These cost serious money without coverage.

## What to look for in a policy

### Medical coverage minimum

For Indonesia, you want **at least $100,000 USD** in medical coverage. Sounds high for a cheap
country, but evacuation to Singapore (the nearest hospital system many policies route to for serious
injuries) costs $20,000–$80,000 alone.

### Adventure sports coverage

This is where most standard travel insurance policies fall short for Indonesia.

"Adventure activities" exclusions vary wildly. Many policies exclude:

- Surfing (categorized as adventure)
- Motorbike riding if you're the driver
- River activities (relevant for Green Canyon and Citumang)

Read this section carefully. If a policy excludes surfing and you plan to surf, that policy is
not covering your most likely injury scenario.

Some policies include adventure sports as standard; others offer it as a paid add-on. SafetyWing
(popular among nomads) covers surfing as standard; World Nomads covers it in their Explorer tier.

### Evacuation coverage

Indonesia's regional hospitals are functional for straightforward cases. For anything complex —
serious neurological injury, cardiac events, anything requiring specialist care — you may need
evacuation to Singapore or Australia. **Medical evacuation is not optional coverage.** It should
be in any policy you buy.

### Trip cancellation

Less critical for long-stay nomads (if you're staying a month, there's less to cancel). More
important if you have flights and bookings that cost real money.

## What nomads in Batukaras actually use

Based on conversations with the nomad-adjacent community I've met here:

**SafetyWing** — the most common choice. Monthly billing ($56.28/month as of 2026), covers
adventure activities including surfing, worldwide (except home country). Medical coverage up to
$250,000. Not perfect — the deductible is $250 and hospital pre-authorization can be slow — but
it's designed for people without a fixed home country and it's honest about what it covers.

The main complaint: customer service response time when you're in a rural hospital at 10 PM is
not great. They get there eventually. Keep the emergency number saved.

**World Nomads** — more expensive ($80–150+/month depending on plan and nationality), better
customer service reputation, more comprehensive. Explorer plan covers surfing and motorbike riding.
A better choice if you're only in Indonesia for 2–4 weeks and cost is less of a factor.

**Annual travel insurance (home country):** A significant chunk of nomads from the UK, Germany,
and Australia have comprehensive annual policies from their home insurers. If you have one, check
specifically whether it covers adventure activities and whether it has a duration cap per trip.

## The motorbike clause — read this carefully

Many policies cover you as a motorbike passenger but **not as the driver** unless you have a valid
motorbike license. In Indonesia, this means either an Indonesian SIM (Surat Izin Mengemudi) or an
international driving permit with motorcycle class.

Most tourists and nomads who rent scooters in Batukaras do not have the correct license. If you
crash while driving a scooter unlicensed, some insurers will deny your medical claim.

The practical situation: this is rarely enforced in Indonesia itself (you'll almost never be asked
for your license), but it matters at claim time. Check your specific policy. Some explicitly allow
scooter riding; others don't.

<InfoBox type="tip">
  An international driving permit with motorcycle class takes 1–2 days to get in most countries
  before you travel. It costs $15–20. It's worth having.
</InfoBox>

## Filing a claim in Indonesia

If something happens:

1. **Document everything.** Photos of the accident scene, hospital receipts, medical reports in
   English if possible (ask the hospital — many have English-language discharge summaries).
2. **Contact your insurer immediately** — don't wait until you're home.
3. **Keep every receipt.** Even the pharmacy stop for antibiotics. Itemize everything.
4. **Get a police report** for accidents — some insurers require it for claims above a certain amount.

The nearest police post to Batukaras is in Cijulang. The hospital in Pangandaran town will issue a
medical report on request.

## What this guide doesn't cover

- Long-term health insurance for people on work visas (different product entirely)
- BPJS — Indonesia's national health insurance system (not accessible to most short-stay foreigners)
- Specific policy reviews (terms change; always read the current version)
